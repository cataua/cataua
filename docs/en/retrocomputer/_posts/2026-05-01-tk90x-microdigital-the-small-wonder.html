The TK90X, produced by Microdigital in the 1980s, is a fascinating piece of computing history. As a Brazilian clone of the ZX Spectrum, it played a crucial role in making home computing accessible to many people in Brazil during that era.

The TK90X featured a Z80A CPU running at 3.5 MHz, 48 KB of RAM, and a unique design that set it apart from its British counterpart.

Programming the TK90X in Z80 Assembly language is a rewarding experience for retro computing enthusiasts. It allows you to explore the inner workings of the machine and create software that runs directly on the hardware.

Whether you're interested in game development, software engineering, or simply want to relive the nostalgia of 8-bit computing, the TK90X offers a unique platform to experiment and create.

In this article, we will delve into the history of the TK90X, its technical specifications, and how to get started with programming it in Z80 Assembly. We'll also share some resources and tips for those interested in exploring this classic machine.
